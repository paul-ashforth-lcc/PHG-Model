PUBLIC HEALTH GRANT ALLOCATION MODELExecutive Methodology & Domain Technical GuideDocument DetailsTarget Audience: Senior Leadership, Elected Members, Health & Wellbeing Board, Finance OfficersOrganization: Public Health IntelligenceSubject: 7-Domain Needs-Based Public Health Grant Allocation Framework1. Executive SummaryHistorically, public health funding allocations across local areas relied heavily on historical spend—simply renewing previous budget splits with minor annual adjustments. This approach entrenching geographical funding inequities over time.This model replaces legacy funding allocations with an evidence-based, 7-domain needs-based formula. Instead of assuming every resident requires the same level of public health support, the model evaluates seven distinct demographic, socio-economic, and geographical drivers of health demand.By adjusting policy sliders across these seven domains, decision-makers can test different strategic priorities—such as prioritizing urban deprivation, rural delivery costs, or healthy aging—while viewing the immediate financial impact on each authority in real time.2. The 7 Domain DriversEach domain calculates an authority’s unweighted percentage share of the total countywide burden for a specific health indicator.Domain 1: Base Population (Per-Capita Baseline)What it measures: The total resident population in each ward using ONS mid-year estimates.Why it matters: Establishes the core headcount requirement. If an authority contains 72.9% of the total county population, its unweighted base population share is 72.9%.Mathematical Formula:Base Population Share = Authority Population / Total County PopulationDomain 2: Deprivation (Index of Multiple Deprivation - IMD)What it measures: The concentration of relative poverty, ill health, and social disadvantage from official English Indices of Deprivation data.Why it matters: Deprivation is the single strongest predictor of early death, long-term illness, and demand for preventative public health services (e.g., addiction recovery, smoking cessation, and health checks).Mathematical Formula: Ward IMD scores are multiplied by ward populations to calculate total Person-Deprivation Units, ensuring large deprived wards carry proportional weight:IMD Share = Sum of (Ward Population × Ward IMD Score) / Countywide Deprivation VolumeDomain 3: 0–19 Age Profile (Children & Young People)What it measures: The total count of residents aged 0 to 19 years.Why it matters: Statutory public health duties include mandatory early years services, including health visiting, school nursing, breastfeeding support, and national child measurement programs.Mathematical Formula:0–19 Age Share = Authority Population Aged 0–19 / Total County Population Aged 0–19Domain 4: 65+ & Frailty Index (Older Population)What it measures: The total count of residents aged 65 and over.Why it matters: Older populations place higher demand on physical activity programs, fall prevention services, social isolation interventions, and integrated health and social care.Mathematical Formula:65+ Frailty Share = Authority Population Aged 65+ / Total County Population Aged 65+Domain 5: Wider Determinants of Health (Housing & Environment)What it measures: Structural environment factors, including housing quality, overcrowding, fuel poverty, and ambient living conditions.Why it matters: Health is largely built outside clinical settings. Cold, damp homes and poor living conditions directly increase respiratory illness and winter hospital admissions.Mathematical Formula: Ward-level wider determinant scores are population-weighted to establish each authority’s relative environmental risk burden.Domain 6: Sparsity & Rurality (Cost of Delivery Factor)What it measures: Population density and geographical dispersion across rural communities.Why it matters: Delivering public health programs across large, sparsely populated rural areas costs significantly more per head due to staff travel times, smaller clinic sizes, and limited public transport.Mathematical Formula: Assigns a rurality factor based on population spread, providing rural authorities with a higher proportional share under this specific factor.Domain 7: Coastal & Seasonal IndexWhat it measures: Geographical coastal strip wards (such as Skegness, Mablethorpe, and Ingoldmells).Why it matters: Coastal communities face unique "seaside deprivation" challenges, including high seasonal population surges, transient populations, and high rates of chronic health conditions among retirees.Mathematical Formula: Identifies coastal strip wards and allocates the coastal domain share proportionally to the authorities containing those communities.3. Weight Normalization: The user adjusts slider weights in the model (e.g., 25% Population, 25% IMD, 10% Age 0–19, etc.). The sum of all weights equals 100%.Composite Score Calculation: For each authority, individual domain shares are multiplied by their respective slider weights and summed:Composite Target Share = Sum of (Domain Share × Domain Weight)Formula Target Allocation: The composite target share is applied directly to the total grant pool (e.g., £35,000,000):Formula Target (£) = Composite Target Share × Total Public Health Grant (£)Transition Smoothing (Damping): If switching to the new formula causes a sharp budget shift compared to historical spending, a damping cap (e.g., ±10%) can be enabled. This caps maximum annual gains or losses, ensuring local services have time to transition without sudden operational disruptions.5.
